The 8-puzzle

Eight numbered tiles sit in a 3 × 3 frame with one empty square. Any tile beside the empty square can slide into it. Starting from a scrambled initial state, the goal is to reach a target goal state.

Searching for the moves is a classic AI exercise. There are 9!/2 = 181,440 reachable arrangements: small enough to search completely, but large enough that the choice of strategy makes a huge difference.

Formulated as a search problem

StatePosition of the eight tiles and the blank
Initial stateTyped in, chosen from examples, or generated at random
ActionsSlide the blank Up, Down, Left or Right (2 to 4 options)
TransitionSwap the blank with the neighbouring tile
Goal testBoard equals the goal board
Path costEach move costs 1

A goal is reachable only when both boards have the same inversion parity. The app checks this before every search.

Why Depth First Search behaves the way it does

01

It uses a stack

The newest state is always expanded next, so the search keeps extending the same line of moves.

02

It backtracks

When a line runs out of unexplored states, DFS pops back to the most recent branch it skipped.

03

It isn't optimal

The first goal it reaches is usually thousands of moves deep, even when 5 moves would do. The Benchmark page shows this.
